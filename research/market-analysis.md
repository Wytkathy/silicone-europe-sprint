# European Market Decision

## Decision

**Initial market:** Germany  
**Category:** reusable silicone food-storage and kitchen organization products  
**Initial account type:** design-led kitchenware brands, sustainable homeware
retailers, and retailers with private-label potential

This is a training hypothesis, not a confirmed commercial recommendation.

## Category Comparison

| Category | ODM/OEM fit | Differentiation room | Compliance burden | Buyer accessibility | Training verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Kitchen and food storage | High | Medium–high | Medium | High | Start here |
| Baby feeding | High | High | High | Medium | Later, after compliance capability is verified |
| Pet accessories | Medium–high | Medium | Medium | High | Useful second experiment |

Kitchen storage is selected because form, color, packaging, and range design can
be demonstrated visually, while the buyer universe includes both brands and
retailers. Before real outreach, verify all food-contact compliance, testing,
traceability, and manufacturing claims with the factory.

## Why Germany

- Large, mature European consumer market with visible sustainability-oriented
  retail and product-design segments.
- A useful test market for clear technical documentation and trust-focused B2B
  communication.
- Many target accounts expose product ranges and positioning publicly, making
  desk research practical.

These statements are working assumptions. Validate them with customer
interviews, trade data, and actual response rates before investing heavily.

## Ideal Customer Profile

- Germany or nearby European market
- Sells kitchen, home organization, reusable, or design-led consumer products
- Has an existing product-development, sourcing, private-label, or wholesale
  motion
- Uses distinct colors, forms, sets, or packaging that could benefit from ODM
- Can plausibly support a multi-SKU range rather than a single commodity item

## Buyer Roles

| Role | Primary concern | Relevant value proposition |
|---|---|---|
| Category buyer | Margin, availability, range fit | A coherent customizable range with clear commercial options |
| Product developer | Feasibility, testing, iteration | Structured sampling and documented design decisions |
| Brand lead | Distinctiveness, consistency | Color, shape, packaging, and story aligned to the brand |
| Quality/compliance | Evidence, traceability, risk | Verified test reports and controlled change documentation |

## Account Scoring Model

Score each field from 1–5.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence question |
|---|---:|---|
| Category fit | 30% | Do they sell relevant kitchen/storage products? |
| ODM/private-label signal | 20% | Is custom product development commercially plausible? |
| Design differentiation | 20% | Do they compete on color, form, usability, or packaging? |
| Sustainability alignment | 15% | Is reuse, longevity, or waste reduction visible? |
| Access and timing | 15% | Is there a reachable team and a current product signal? |

`Weighted score = Σ(score × weight)`. A high score is still only a prioritization
hypothesis, not proof of buying intent.

## Evidence Rules

- Record the official URL supporting the account's category or positioning.
- Label inferred sourcing opportunities as `hypothesis`.
- Never claim an account needs a supplier without direct evidence.
- Re-check facts immediately before outreach.
